# Melee

The 1.50 Melee mode: a short two-player tactical contest on a tiny map rather than a normal campaign. Players build fleets from identical starts and fight them out in tactical combat.

## Rules

A complete rule set of its own, built on 1.31 with many changes; it does not include the Basic or Improved packages. Choose a Small galaxy.

## What it changes

Races and start:
- Every race is the same: no race picks to spend, every stock race is a Dictatorship with Stealthy Ships (which costs nothing), and Dictatorship has no morale penalty or barracks bonus.
- Each player starts with 2 population, 10 BC, no marines and no starting ships.
- Starting colonies get a Star Fortress, Battlestation, Star Base and Robotic Factory where the technology allows.
- If you start at Post-warp, every player receives the same fixed set of melee technologies.

Galaxy:
- Galaxy sizes have 4, 8, 16 or 32 stars (Small to Huge).
- Every star has three planets, all Abundant; no black holes, nebulas or wormholes, and the only planet specials are gold and gem deposits.
- The stars around each home system are revealed from the start.

Economy:
- Every planet holds a base of 2 population, each worker produces 60 production, and a colony's first 60 production causes no pollution.
- A colony can complete several items in one turn when its production allows.
- Command points: 10 at the start, 10 per Star Base and 10 from Hyperspace Communications; a ship uses 1, 2, 6, 10, 15 or 30 points from Frigate to Doom Star.
- No random events and no officers offered for hire before turn 127; in Average-tech games the Antarans come later.
- No research prompt at the start; scientists produce 1 research each instead of 3.

Technology and ships:
- The technology tree is rebuilt for melee: many technologies are free from the start, others are removed.
- Hulls, armor, shields, computers, weapons, missile racks and special systems all get melee values; weapons and special systems cost nothing, and hulls are much cheaper.
- Class III and Class V Shields are renamed Class II and Class III.
- Crews gain no experience each turn and crew levels give no combat bonuses.
- Tactical combat uses the 1.50 Improved rules: strict ship initiative, live statistics, missiles moving at the end of the turn, 1-square boarding, and the simplified beam formula.
- Combat confirmation messages close after about 5 seconds, and boarding results close on their own.
- AI ship designs are the Improved ones.

## Keys

| Key | Screen | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| W | Tactical combat | Each ship can wait only once per combat turn. Ships that waited act afterwards, in reverse initiative order, and W is not available during that second pass. |
| H | Tactical combat | Waits with all your ships. Ship initiative is always on (the in-game Ship Initiative option can't switch it off), so H is there in every battle until the waiting ships start their second pass. |
| Alt+A, Alt+D | Build | Auto Design still redesigns every ship, but follows Melee's AI design rules, so the designs differ. |

## Needs and conflicts

No dependencies. Conflicts with Improved (v150i) and Version 1.2 (v120). Both players need the same package set.

## Saves

Saves record this package and need it enabled to load.
